Mara is back on Elm Street at ten to one with the tank a quarter down. The
car has to come back to the bay it left from; a car left anywhere else is
treated as still out, the booking keeps running, and the member pays an
hour for every hour until a volunteer has fetched it, on top of the cost of
fetching. She parks, the screen asks for the fuel level again and for the
odometer, and she locks the car from the app. The booking ends when the car
is locked in its bay, not when the booked hour arrives: finishing early
ends it early, and she pays for the hours she booked, not the hours she
used, because the car was hers for all of them.

Running late is different. If Mara is still out at one, the booking runs
on in half-hour steps at one and a half times the daytime rate, and the
member who has the car next is told at once that it is late and by how
much. If the next booking starts before Mara is back, the share offers that
member another car at no extra cost and charges the difference to Mara.
Three late returns in a year earn the same conversation with the committee
that three absences do.

Fuel is the share's. A car returned with less than a quarter of a tank asks
the member to fill it on the next trip or marks it for a volunteer; a
member who fills the tank is credited the receipt against their next
statement, as long as the receipt is photographed through the app on the
same day. Every trip's distance is the difference between the odometer
readings at the start and the end, and the twenty kilometres included with
each booked hour are summed over the booking before any excess is charged.

At the end of the month Mara gets one statement for every booking she made
in it: hours booked, hours late, kilometres over the allowance, fuel
credits, and the cancellation and absence charges if any. The statement is
settled from the card on her account on the fifth of the following month;
a payment that fails is tried again a week later, and a second failure
suspends her bookings until the balance is paid.
