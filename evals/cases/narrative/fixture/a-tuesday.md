Mara needs the car on Tuesday. She opens the app on Monday evening, picks
the blue estate parked on Elm Street, and asks for it from nine until one.
The share answers at once: the car is free, the booking is hers, and it
tells her the price before she confirms — four hours at the daytime rate,
with the first twenty kilometres included and every kilometre past that
charged at the end. A booking is confirmed only after she has seen the
price; nothing is charged until the trip ends.

Had someone else wanted the estate from eleven, the share would have
refused Mara's request rather than shorten it, and offered her the hatchback
two streets over for the same hours. Two bookings never overlap on one car,
and the share never resizes a request on a member's behalf. A booking can
be made as little as fifteen minutes ahead and as far ahead as sixty days,
and it can run for any whole number of hours between one and seventy-two.

Mara can cancel until an hour before the booking starts without paying.
Cancelling inside that last hour costs her one hour at the booked rate,
because the car has by then been held against other members for the better
part of a day. A member who simply does not turn up pays for the whole
booking, and after three such absences in a year the share stops accepting
bookings from that member until someone on the committee has spoken to
them.

On Tuesday morning Mara walks to Elm Street. The car unlocks from the app
from ten minutes before her booking starts, and only for her. Inside, the
screen asks her to confirm the fuel level and to photograph any damage she
can see before she drives; whatever she records is attached to the booking,
and damage found by the next member is attributed to the last booking that
did not record it. She notes a scuff on the rear bumper, and drives.
