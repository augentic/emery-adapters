Before any of this, Mara had to join. The share is open to anyone over
twenty-one who lives within the four postcodes it serves and has held a
full driving licence for at least two years. She applied in the app with
her licence, a proof of address no older than three months, and a card for
payment; a volunteer checked the documents within two working days and
approved her. Until she was approved she could browse the cars and their
calendars but not book, and the application told her so rather than
letting her try.

Membership runs for a year from approval and renews unless she cancels it
a month before the anniversary. A member whose licence expires, or who
picks up six or more penalty points, loses the right to book on the day
the share learns of it and gets it back once a volunteer has seen the new
licence or the points have fallen below six. A member who moves out of the
four postcodes keeps her membership until the anniversary and is not
renewed.

Each member has one account and one card on it, and the card is charged
only for the monthly statement; nothing is taken at booking time. Mara can
add a second driver to her account — her partner, who passed the same
checks — and bookings made by either appear on the one statement. A second
driver cannot add drivers of their own, and removing the second driver
takes effect at once but does not cancel bookings they have already made,
which stay on Mara's statement.

The committee is the five members who run the share. A committee member
can suspend a member's bookings, lift a suspension, approve an application,
and close a car for repairs; every one of those actions is recorded with
who did it and when, and a suspended member is told why in writing on the
day. Nothing a committee member does changes a statement already issued.
