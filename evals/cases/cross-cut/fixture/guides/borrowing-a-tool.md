# Borrowing a tool

Depends on: [loans](../reference/loans.md), [fair use](../policies/fair-use.md).

## At the desk

Bring the tool from the shelf to the desk with your membership card, or
show the card in the app. The volunteer scans the tool's label and your
card; the app checks that your membership is current, that you have room
under your loan limit, that nothing you have out is overdue, and that you
have no unpaid charge over ten pounds. If any of those fail the loan is
refused and the screen says which, so the volunteer can tell you what to
do about it. If they pass, the app proposes a due date — a week from today
by default, shorter for tools the catalogue marks as high-demand — and
the volunteer confirms it with you. The loan starts when the volunteer
confirms; from then until the tool is scanned back in it is yours and your
responsibility.

Some tools come in a case with accessories: drill bits, sanding sheets, a
spare blade, a charger. The catalogue page lists what should be in the
case, and the volunteer checks the contents with you before the loan
starts so that anything already missing is recorded against the previous
loan rather than yours. A tool whose case is incomplete can still be lent
if what is missing does not stop it working; the catalogue page is updated
at the same time.

## How long you can keep it

A loan is for seven days unless the tool's catalogue page says otherwise.
A few tools — the floor sander, the tile cutter, the pressure washer, the
long ladders — are lent for three days because they are in constant demand
and nobody needs one for a week. The due date is shown on the receipt the
app sends you when the loan starts, and again in a reminder two days before
it falls due.

You can extend a loan once, by up to seven more days, as long as nobody has
reserved the tool. Extending is done in the app or at the desk and takes
effect at once; the new due date replaces the old one and a fresh receipt
is sent. A loan that has already been extended, or whose tool is reserved,
cannot be extended, and the app says which it is. There is no limit on
borrowing the same tool again after returning it, except that a tool with
a waiting reservation goes to the reservation first.

## What you may not do

A borrowed tool is lent to you, not to your household or your street.
Lending it on to someone else — even another member — is a breach of the
membership agreement, and the committee treats damage done while a tool
was lent on as unreported damage by the member who borrowed it. Tools are
for domestic use within the Hollow Lane area; a member who wants a tool for
paid work asks the committee first, and the committee may refuse or may
ask for a deposit.
