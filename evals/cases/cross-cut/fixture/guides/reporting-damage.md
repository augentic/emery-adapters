# Reporting damage

Depends on: [damage and loss](../policies/damage-and-loss.md).

## Tell us as soon as it happens

Tools break. A drill that stops mid-hole, a ladder with a bent rung, a
blade that snaps: none of these is a crime, and reporting them is the
whole of what we ask. Report damage in the app from the loan on your
account page, with a sentence saying what happened and a photograph if you
can take one, as soon as you notice it and in any case before you bring
the tool back. A report made before the return is a reported damage and is
charged, if at all, at the reported rate in the damage and loss policy;
damage found by a volunteer at the return that was not reported is
unreported damage and is charged at the higher unreported rate. The
difference is deliberate: a tool we know is broken can be taken off the
shelf before the next member takes it home and finds out the hard way.

Once you have reported damage the tool's catalogue page is marked "damage
reported" and the tool cannot be reserved or lent until a volunteer has
looked at it. If you still have the tool and it is unsafe — a frayed cable,
a cracked casing, a guard that no longer closes — stop using it and bring
it back at the next session; the due date no longer matters once damage is
reported and no late charge accrues from the report onward.

## Wear is not damage

A tool that has stopped working from age or ordinary use — brushes worn
down, a battery that no longer holds its charge, a chuck that has lost its
grip — is wear, not damage, and nothing is charged for it. Report it the
same way, so the tool can be repaired or retired, and say that you think
it is wear; the volunteer who inspects it decides, and you can dispute the
decision within seven days as you can any condition finding.

## Lost tools

A tool you cannot find is reported the same way, as lost. A lost tool is
charged at the catalogue's replacement value under the damage and loss
policy, and the charge is refunded if the tool turns up and is returned
within four weeks of the report. A tool lost from a case — a bit, a blade,
a charger — is charged at the part's value, which the catalogue page
lists, and not at the whole tool's.

## Stolen tools

If a tool is stolen from you, report it as lost and give us the police
reference. The committee waives the replacement charge for a tool stolen
from a locked house or a locked car when there is a police reference; a
tool taken from an unlocked shed, a van overnight, or a front garden is
treated as lost.
