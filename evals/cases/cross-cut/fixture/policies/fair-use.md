# Fair use policy

Set by the committee; last changed 2025-11-08. Depends on:
[loans](../reference/loans.md), [catalogue](../reference/catalogue.md).

Four hundred tools serve about a thousand members, and a few tools serve
nearly all of them. The fair-use rules keep any one member from holding
more of the shelf than their share, and keep the tools everyone wants
moving.

## The loan limit

A member may have three tools out at once. A member in their first month
after approval may have one. The limit counts open loans, not reservations
or holds, and a tool returned at the desk frees its place in the same
session. Volunteers may, at the desk and for a stated reason — a member
clearing a house, a community event — lend a fourth tool for one loan
period; the exception is recorded on the loan.

## High-demand tools

Tools the catalogue marks as high-demand are lent for three days instead
of seven. They count double toward the loan limit, and a member may have
only one of them out at a time. The committee decides which tools are
high-demand from the length of their reservation queues, reviewed at each
meeting; the current list is on each tool's catalogue page and on the
noticeboard. A tool taken off the high-demand list reverts to a seven-day
loan for loans opened after the change, not for loans already open.

## Borrowing the same tool again

There is no limit on how often a member borrows one tool, but a tool with
an open reservation goes to the reservation when it is returned and not
back to the member who had it, and a member may not reserve a tool they
currently have out. A member who returns a tool and borrows it again in
the same session is making a new loan, with a new due date and no
extension carried over.

## Domestic use

Tools are lent for domestic use at the member's own home or another
address in the four postcodes. A member who wants a tool for paid work, or
for use outside the area, asks the committee first; the committee may
agree, may ask for a deposit of the tool's replacement value, or may
refuse. Lending a borrowed tool on to anyone else, member or not, is a
breach of the membership agreement; damage done while a tool was lent on
is unreported damage by the member who borrowed it, whoever caused it.

## Unpaid charges

A member whose unpaid charges exceed ten pounds may not open a new loan
until the balance is under ten pounds; a disputed charge does not count
toward the ten pounds until the committee decides it. Reservations are not
blocked by unpaid charges, but a hold cannot be collected while a loan is
blocked.
