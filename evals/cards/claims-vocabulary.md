# The claims-vocabulary card

A round of the eval, run and read under [the protocol](README.md#protocol); [the README](README.md#rounds) indexes every round.

The boundary card's rerun (`2026-10-11T23-43-20Z`, [the build arm under the boundary](node.md#the-build-arm-under-the-boundary)) left thirteen `nestjs-pair` requirements no slice covered, every one `status: unknown` and from one source, and the first reading took them for requirements stated in the platform's vocabulary — `AuthGuard('jwt')`, `JwtPayloadType`, "via `FileInterceptor`" — which no target on another platform can cover. The SDK's `claims.md` holds the id grammar, the anchors, the skip roots, and the gate, and nothing on what a requirement states, so the seam is each adapter's `extract.md` in this repository. The runner's `mechanism` column was written first, then the thirteen were read back, and the card's arm is one sentence in two prompts. [The protocol](README.md#protocol); `composer-2.5`, three specify-only runs per arm over `nestjs-pair` and `express-orders`, the binary the boundary card's rerun used.

## The figure

`mechanism` is `named/cited` per source: the requirements whose body names a platform's code identifier the design owns no type for — a decorator, a call, or a type's name in CamelCase, in backticks or bare, since `documentation`'s model sets code apart and `typescript`'s does not — over the requirements citing the source ([evals/README.md](../README.md) has the shapes). The program's own vocabulary is left out by shape: a field, an error key, a configuration key, a function of the program's own. The first cut counted those too and read `code 100/187` on the rerun's `nestjs-pair`, every `firstName`, `errors.email`, and `auth.secret` in the specification, which says nothing of the platform; the shapes kept read `docs 14/50 · code 17/187`, and `express-orders` `2/89` (`ZodError`, `redis.ping()`). The list under the rerun's `nestjs-pair`: on the docs side the product names (`NestJS`, `MongoDB`, `PostgreSQL`, `TypeORM` — REQ-001, 004, 005, 037, 039), the Passport strategy (`validate()`, `UnauthorizedException`, `request.user` — REQ-016, 017), the guards (REQ-018, 019), class-transformer's interceptor and decorators (REQ-045 to 048), and `I18nContext.current()` (REQ-050); on the code side `ValidationPipe` and `UnprocessableEntityException` (REQ-056 to 058), TypeORM and `DataSource` (REQ-064 to 067, 203), `I18nModule` and `HeaderResolver` (REQ-070), `SerializeOptions` (REQ-129), `FileInterceptor` (REQ-144, 156), S3's `PutObject`, `GetObject`, and `ContentLength` (REQ-153, 154, 158), and `AuthGuard('jwt')` (REQ-161). A product name has the shape and stays in: naming the platform is the same statement.

## The thirteen, read back

Each of the thirteen to its claim in the rerun's `run-1.evidence-*.json` and the line it anchors, no turn spent:

- **Two are guidance to the code's author**, stating nothing a caller observes: REQ-018 (`auth.md#L163`, the request typed `JwtPayloadType` under `AuthGuard('jwt')`) and REQ-020 (`#L192`, "always pass the payload type matching the guard").
- **Five state a behaviour through the platform's piece**: REQ-021 (`#L194`, `RolesGuard` tolerates an absent `user` — a role check on an unauthenticated route does not fail for want of one); REQ-144 and REQ-156 (`@UseInterceptors(FileInterceptor('file'))` — one multipart field named `file`); REQ-152 (`randomStringGenerator()` and the lowercased extension as the stored key); REQ-153 (S3's `PutObject` with `ContentLength`, expiring in 3600 s — a service's vocabulary rather than the platform's, which a target on any platform calls by those names).
- **Six are platform-neutral behaviours the target left out of `covered`**: REQ-023 (the token in the `Authorization` header), REQ-027 (the rolling thirty-day idle window), REQ-034 (social login cannot inherit an unconfirmed account), and REQ-143, 148, 155 (an upload requires a valid JWT, anchored at `@UseGuards(AuthGuard('jwt'))` and stated without it). These are the target's, a finding for a `node` card, recorded and not pursued here.

So the premise held for seven of thirteen, and the figure holds four of them (REQ-018, 144, 153, 156): the others name a design type (`JwtPayloadType`, `RolesGuard`, left out by construction), a lowerCamel utility (`randomStringGenerator`, the program's-own shape under the figure's rule), or no identifier at all. The third class — genuinely platform-specific, with nothing to restate — is the boilerplate's own: REQ-001, 004, 005, and 037, its architecture (`[SCHEMA].ts` for MongoDB, `[ENTITY].ts` for PostgreSQL) and its database offering ("PostgreSQL with TypeORM and MongoDB with Mongoose"), four of 221, the case's rather than the prose's, since `nestjs-pair`'s docs are a framework boilerplate's guide. `expected.toml` is not marked: the runner reads no such field, the figure's list names the four on every run, and four requirements move no column; the decision is revisited if the on arm's residue is these four alone.

## The arm

One sentence in each prompt, on what a requirement states and nothing else:

- `sources/typescript/prose/extract.md`, after "Lift every behavioural fact into a `requirement`": *A statement names what the system does in the system's own terms — the field, the status, the key, the limit, the order of events — and never the framework's or library's piece that does it, which the excerpt keeps: `accepts one multipart form field named file`, not `via FileInterceptor`; `requires a valid access token before the handler runs`, not `runs behind AuthGuard('jwt')`.*
- `sources/documentation/prose/extract.md`, after "A requirement's statement is the rule whole": *A statement is the behaviour a caller observes, in the system's own terms, not the framework piece a passage prescribes to the author — `a property marked hidden is absent from every response`, not `use @Exclude({ toPlainOnly: true })` — and a passage that only tells the author which type, annotation, or import to use, stating nothing a caller observes, is no requirement.*

The off arm is HEAD's two components (`59c66fe`'s, unchanged since); the on arm is built from a detached worktree at HEAD with the two sentences; both run under the same `EMERY_BIN` (the rerun's `emery 0.38.0`), `CURSOR_MODEL=composer-2.5`, `EVAL_RUNS=3`, over `nestjs-pair` and `express-orders`, specify only.

- Observed, not predicted: tokens, correction rounds, wall; and the `req` count per source, since the sentence may merge or drop.

## Decision rule

The two sentences ship in the working tree when V1 and V3 hold on at least two of three runs per case and V2 holds on every run. V1 holding while V3 fails is a drop, not a rewrite: nothing ships, and the sentence is rewritten before another arm. V2 failing on any column is recorded against the sentence and nothing ships. An expectation moves only where the code's own rule says the line was wrong, and no expectation is in question here.
