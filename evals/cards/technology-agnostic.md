# The technology-agnostic specification

A programme of the eval, run and read under [the protocol](README.md#protocol); [the README](README.md#rounds) indexes every round.

## The technology lexicon

The `train` case's specification carried the source's technology into its requirements and stems: `useConfluentKafkaConfig` and `KeyVault` in requirement statements, `key-vault` as a stem, `start.confluent-secret-before-kafka` as an id. A requirement's body is the winning claim's `statement` verbatim, so the engine never rewrites it, and the stem is locked at the survey turn, since the extraction gate then holds every id to it. The arm is one lexicon applied as a correction finding at each of the three turns that author specification text — the SDK's fixed vendor, product, protocol, framework, and host names (`emery_adapter::Lexicon`, emery 0.38.0), and beside them the terms of the packages a seam's modules import (the SDK's survey and extraction gates) or the run's `call` claims spell (the engine's spec-draft gate): a survey stem naming one is returned to rename before extraction locks it, a `requirement` or `criterion` id or body naming one is returned to restate by role, and a spec-draft scenario line or preamble paragraph naming one is returned the same way; the design turn is exempt, since the technology is its to carry, and a `decision` claim informs `## APIs and integrations` as a `call` does. In this repository, the code adapters' `extract.md` carve the technology out of the source's own name for a behaviour (`start.credentials-before-clients`, not `start.confluent-secret-before-kafka`), their `survey.md` name a service surface for its role (`class KeyVault` → `secrets`), and the prose adapters gain `decision` for the directive or passage that prescribes a technology, the behaviour it governs a `requirement` by role, so a brief saying `publish each order event to Kafka` is two claims.

The runner reads the arm on a new column, `leaks`: the places the specification's own words name a technology under the lexicon the engine's spec gate derives — each accepted `requirement` and `criterion` claim's id and body, then the committed specification's preamble paragraphs and scenario lines — expected `0`, each listed in the notes as `- leak: <where> names <names>`. The figure stands beside `mechanism`, which reads identifiers by shape and keeps counting a design-owned type's name wherever it is spelled. A new case, `orders-broker`, was written to leak: two decision records choosing the broker (`Kafka on Confluent Cloud`) and the secret store (`Azure Key Vault`) by name, beside a small `typescript` service whose identifiers spell them — `useConfluentKafkaConfig`, `ConfluentSecretRetriever`, `KeyVault`, `kafkajs`, `@azure/keyvault-secrets` — the `train` shape at a tenth of the size, so the bootstrap's credential steps, the retriever only it reaches, and the one service module are all present and every requirement has a role to be stated by. Its `--facts` pass lays the bootstrap `src/start.ts`, the one registration `router.post("/orders")` at `src/orders.ts#L25-L43`, and `src/key-vault.ts` exporting `KeyVault` through `@azure/keyvault-secrets` and `@azure/identity` as the service module, as the expectation reads them.

The round is a fresh `specify` of three cases — `cargo run -p evals -- orders-broker train train-pair`, three runs each under `CURSOR_MODEL` pinned to `composer-2.5`, the release `emery` and the components built from the two working trees that carry the arm — read on the `leaks` column, the `stems` and `surfaces` columns, and `recall dec` against the cards since. The predictions and the rule were written before the run; the runs are the operator's.

### Predictions

- **`leaks` 0 on every run of every case.** Each gate holds its turn under the same fixed names, and the run's package terms are a subset of each seam's where the seam imports the package, so a leak is a name the lexicon lacks, or a term of one seam's packages spelled by another seam's claim. The notes name it either way; a false positive — a domain word the lexicon holds, which the fixed list leaves out by design (`express`, `vault`, `rabbit`) — is read as a correction round the extraction spent, not a leak, since a corrected claim is not in the accepted evidence.
- **`orders-broker`.** `recall dec` 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs, each record's prescription verbatim with the technology in it. The code's `stems` exact — `start`, `orders`, `secrets` — and `surfaces` 3/3, the secret-store surface at `src/key-vault.ts` under `secrets` by `survey.md`'s own example, after one correction round where the first answer named it `key-vault` (the `survey-code` completion `corrected`), or none where the prompt's rule held at the first answer. The docs' `stems` the model's: `orders` and `secrets`, or `events` for the first record, read as a band. Behaviours 5/5 on ≥ 2/3 runs with the one split the case allows (the record's one refusal sentence against the code's two branches); `mechanism` 0 for `code`, since the retriever and the vault are the design's `mechanism` types and no requirement names them, and 0 for `docs`. The `start` requirements under `code` fewer than the six planted — the credential steps stated once by role (`start.credentials-before-clients`) rather than once per identifier — so recall req within 11–15 of 15.
- **`train`.** `stems` `-1 +1`: the expectation's `key-vault` is the technology's name, which the gate now returns, and the arm's stem for `src/key-vault.ts` is `secrets`; the `[[source.surface]]` and the `stem = ["key-vault", "start"]` requirement read as restemmed and misplaced on every run, and the expectation moves to `secrets` with this card, by the stem rule's own reading. No card has graded `train` yet, so its row is the first and is read by its misses by anchor rather than against a band: of the 32 requirements planted, the misses fall on the `start` anchors written under the retriever's and the clients' names (`src/start.ts#L11-L13`, `#L21-L34`, `#L36-L48`, `#L51-L58`, `src/confluent-secret-retriever.ts#L14-L29`, `#L33-L41`), stated once by role where they were planted once per identifier, and nowhere under `train-update`. Of the 8 criteria, the two naming `KEY_VAULT_SECRET_NAME_CONFLUENT_KEY` and the Confluent default under `IS_LOCAL` are met at the same anchors, restated by role (`the credential secret's name`), since a criterion's anchor is graded and its words are not.
- **`train-pair`.** As `train` for its `train` source; the `connector` source's stems unchanged, since `inbound` and `start` name no technology; the byte-equal pre-merges across the two `start`s within the band the omnia cards read.
- **Every case.** `mechanism` falls where it counted a product's name (`TypeORM`, `KeyVault`) and holds where it counted a decorator or a call, since the lexicon reads names and `mechanism` reads shape. The `design blocks (types)` count holds, the design turn being exempt. No run fails in an engine turn on a correction the gate cannot settle: the spec-draft's correction rounds are observed on the `spec-draft` completion's outcome.

### Decision rule

- **The arm holds** when `leaks` is 0 on every run of the three cases, `orders-broker`'s `recall dec` is 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs, and no `code` stem of any case spells a lexicon name. The `train` expectation then moves to `secrets` and this card records the move; the `leaks` column is the band every later arm over a prompt reads against, and the `mechanism` column is read for identifiers by shape alone.
- **A gate is short** when a `leaks` note names a fixed name on ≥ 2/3 runs of a case: the lexicon lacks it or `names` misreads a spelling, and the fix is the entry or the word rule, recorded here with the note. A note naming a package term alone — one seam's package in another seam's claim — is the extraction's and the fix is the seam's `lexicon`, which the SDK fills from the seam's own packages.
- **The carve-out is unread** when `orders-broker`'s `start` or `train`'s `start` recall falls below its band because a requirement the model would have stated under the identifier's name was dropped rather than restated: the correction finding tells the model to restate, and a dropped claim is a prompt the finding's wording did not carry; the fix is the finding's text in the SDK, recorded here.
- **`decision` does not land** when `orders-broker`'s `recall dec` is below 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs: the `documentation` prompt's `decision` row is not where the model reads a prescribing passage from, and the next arm is a worked example of a record whose `## Decision` names a product, in `extract.md`.
