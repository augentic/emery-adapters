# The technology-agnostic specification

A programme of the eval, run and read under [the protocol](README.md#protocol); [the README](README.md#rounds) indexes every round.

## The technology lexicon

The `train` case's specification carried the source's technology into its requirements and stems: `useConfluentKafkaConfig` and `KeyVault` in requirement statements, `key-vault` as a stem, `start.confluent-secret-before-kafka` as an id. A requirement's body is the winning claim's `statement` verbatim, so the engine never rewrites it, and the stem is locked at the survey turn, since the extraction gate then holds every id to it. The arm is one lexicon applied as a correction finding at each of the three turns that author specification text — the SDK's fixed vendor, product, protocol, framework, and host names (`emery_adapter::Lexicon`, emery 0.38.0), and beside them the terms of the packages a seam's modules import (the SDK's survey and extraction gates) or the run's `call` claims spell (the engine's spec-draft gate): a survey stem naming one is returned to rename before extraction locks it, a `requirement` or `criterion` id or body naming one is returned to restate by role, and a spec-draft scenario line or preamble paragraph naming one is returned the same way; the design turn is exempt, since the technology is its to carry, and a `decision` claim informs `## APIs and integrations` as a `call` does. In this repository, the code adapters' `extract.md` carve the technology out of the source's own name for a behaviour (`start.credentials-before-clients`, not `start.confluent-secret-before-kafka`), their `survey.md` state the stem gate (a stem spelling a technology is returned to rename), and the prose adapters gain `decision` for the directive or passage that prescribes a technology, the behaviour it governs a `requirement` by role, so a brief saying `publish each order event to Kafka` is two claims. The round was written before [the business-specification round](business-spec.md) landed and is read after it: a secret retriever or a store client is a mechanism reached through the bootstrap and never a surface, so the stem gate has no service stem to return, and the readiness the lexicon arm would have restated by role (`start.credentials-before-clients`) is the design's and no requirement's, listed `[[absent]]`; what the arm still decides is the stems, ids, and specification text a run writes where a behaviour stands.

The runner reads the arm on a new column, `leaks`: the places the specification's own words name a technology under the lexicon the engine's spec gate derives — each accepted `requirement` and `criterion` claim's id and body, then the committed specification's preamble paragraphs and scenario lines — expected `0`, each listed in the notes as `- leak: <where> names <names>`. The figure stands beside `mechanism`, which reads identifiers by shape and keeps counting a design-owned type's name wherever it is spelled. A new case, `orders-broker`, was written to leak: two decision records choosing the broker (`Kafka on Confluent Cloud`) and the secret store (`Azure Key Vault`) by name, beside a small `typescript` service whose identifiers spell them — `useConfluentKafkaConfig`, `ConfluentSecretRetriever`, `KeyVault`, `kafkajs`, `@azure/keyvault-secrets` — the `train` shape at a tenth of the size, so the bootstrap's credential steps and the retriever and store client only it reaches are all present, listed `[[absent]]`, and the orders handler and the records' rules have a role to be stated by. Its `--facts` pass lays the bootstrap `src/start.ts` and the one registration `router.post("/orders")` at `src/orders.ts#L25-L43`, with `src/key-vault.ts` and `src/confluent-secret-retriever.ts` in `start`'s closure and offered by no fact, as the expectation reads them.

The round is a fresh `specify` of three cases — `cargo run -p evals -- orders-broker train train-pair`, three runs each under `CURSOR_MODEL` pinned to `composer-2.5`, the release `emery` and the components built from the two working trees that carry the arm — read on the `leaks` column, the `stems` and `surfaces` columns, and `recall dec` against the cards since. The predictions and the rule were written before the run; the runs are the operator's.

### Predictions

- **`leaks` 0 on every run of every case.** Each gate holds its turn under the same fixed names, and the run's package terms are a subset of each seam's where the seam imports the package, so a leak is a name the lexicon lacks, or a term of one seam's packages spelled by another seam's claim. The notes name it either way; a false positive — a domain word the lexicon holds, which the fixed list leaves out by design (`express`, `vault`, `rabbit`) — is read as a correction round the extraction spent, not a leak, since a corrected claim is not in the accepted evidence.
- **`orders-broker`.** `recall dec` 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs, each record's prescription verbatim with the technology in it. The code's `stems` exact — `start`, `orders` — and `surfaces` 2/2, neither `src/key-vault.ts` nor the retriever named, since the facts offer them to no surface; a run that names one anyway names it `key-vault` or `secret-retriever` first, and the `survey-code` completion reads `corrected` where the stem gate returned the first, which is the gate's one observation here. The docs' `stems` the model's: `orders` and `secrets`, or `events` for the first record, read as a band. Behaviours 5/5 on ≥ 2/3 runs with the one split the case allows (the record's one refusal sentence against the code's two branches), the three credential behaviours met by the docs alone; `absent hits` 0 on ≥ 2/3 runs over the four readiness spans, at most 1 on the third, at `src/confluent-secret-retriever.ts` where it comes, the refusal to start being the one line of the family that reads as a setting the process refuses to run without; `mechanism` 0 for `code` and for `docs`. Recall req 4/4 under `code` — the one `start` rule and the three `orders` — and 5/5 under `docs`, or 4/5 where the model reads the record's first consequence, the credentials obtained before the client, as the design's.
- **`train`.** The expectation already stands where [the business-specification round](business-spec.md) moved it — `train-update` alone, `start` holding no requirement, `src/key-vault.ts` and the retriever `[[absent]]` — so the arm moves nothing there: `stems` exact and `surfaces` 2/2 as that card's P1 predicts, `absent hits` as its P2, and this round reads `train` on `leaks` alone, 0 on every run, with the `train-update` requirements' ids and statements and the `train` scenario lines where a `Kafka`, a `Confluent`, or a `KeyVault` would have leaked from the identifiers the fixture spells. Of the 6 criteria, the one naming the Confluent default under `IS_LOCAL` is met at its anchor restated by role (`the managed-broker configuration`), since a criterion's anchor is graded and its words are not.
- **`train-pair`.** As `train` for its `train` source; the `connector` source's stems unchanged, since `inbound` and `start` name no technology; the byte-equal pre-merges across the two `start`s within the band the omnia cards read.
- **Every case.** `mechanism` falls where it counted a product's name (`TypeORM`, `KeyVault`) and holds where it counted a decorator or a call, since the lexicon reads names and `mechanism` reads shape. The `design blocks (types)` count holds, the design turn being exempt. No run fails in an engine turn on a correction the gate cannot settle: the spec-draft's correction rounds are observed on the `spec-draft` completion's outcome.

### Decision rule

- **The arm holds** when `leaks` is 0 on every run of the three cases, `orders-broker`'s `recall dec` is 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs, and no `code` stem of any case spells a lexicon name. The `leaks` column is the band every later arm over a prompt reads against, and the `mechanism` column is read for identifiers by shape alone.
- **A gate is short** when a `leaks` note names a fixed name on ≥ 2/3 runs of a case: the lexicon lacks it or `names` misreads a spelling, and the fix is the entry or the word rule, recorded here with the note. A note naming a package term alone — one seam's package in another seam's claim — is the extraction's and the fix is the seam's `lexicon`, which the SDK fills from the seam's own packages.
- **The carve-out is unread** when `orders-broker`'s `orders` or `train`'s `train-update` recall falls below its band because a requirement the model would have stated under the identifier's name was dropped rather than restated: the correction finding tells the model to restate, and a dropped claim is a prompt the finding's wording did not carry; the fix is the finding's text in the SDK, recorded here.
- **`decision` does not land** when `orders-broker`'s `recall dec` is below 2/2 on ≥ 2/3 runs: the `documentation` prompt's `decision` row is not where the model reads a prescribing passage from, and the next arm is a worked example of a record whose `## Decision` names a product, in `extract.md`.
