# Eval cards

Scorecards the eval runner (`cargo run --example eval`, [examples/README.md](../../README.md)) wrote under `target/eval/`, copied here by hand because `target/` is gitignored and `make sweep` drops what sits there untouched for a week. Each is one dated run of the eval over the cases named in its headings, and each records the model, the number of runs a case, and — per run — the exit code, the wall time, the completions the run opened with their tokens, the requirement and criterion recall against the case's `expected.toml`, the stems, and the misses by anchor. The runner keeps writing to `target/eval/`; a card worth keeping is copied in, never edited.

The `emery` and `typescript.wasm` paths a card's header cites are the build the run used — a Cursor sandbox cache of this tree's `cargo-target`, not a path this repository holds — and say nothing beyond which binary answered.

## The survey-by-model experiment

Two arms of the `typescript` adapter over five cases — the four above and `api-routes`, a Next.js app-router API the parser has no rule for — three runs each under `composer-2.5`, the treatment once more under `gpt-5.3-codex`. The control is the parser's survey as shipped. The treatment is the `model-survey` feature: the parser reads the tree as before, then one `survey` turn hands the model what it read — the manifest, the bootstrap, every call that hands a function to a package's receiver, every decorated method, what the entry modules export, the packages imported, the modules laid whole — and the model names each surface, anchors it where the code registers or declares it, and gives it the stem its ids lead with under the convention `survey.md` states; code derives the closure each anchor reaches, the bootstrap's `start`, the ids, the anchors, and the briefs exactly as it does from its own surfaces, so the seams differ in the naming alone. The eval grades what it always graded and, new for this experiment, the `(entry, stem)` pairs the survey decided against each case's `[[surface]]` table, the stem sets across runs, and the pairs every two runs share; a run the backend's cap ends re-runs one rung up `EVAL_LADDER` and the card's `Budget ladder` says where it landed, while the comparison columns stay at the defaults.

The predictions below were written before the arms were built, and the rows after them record what the cards showed against each.

### Predictions

- **Control, `composer-2.5` × 3.** The four existing cases: stems `exact` on every run, recall at the `12-54-15Z` floor — `cli-jobs` 17/17, `express-orders` ≥ 29/30, `orders` 6/6, `r9k` ≥ 28/32 — and surfaces exact, since the parser decides them the same way on every run. `api-routes`: the parser finds no bootstrap (no `main`, no conventional entry that runs anything) and no registration, so `exported()` falls to the modules nothing imports — the four route modules — and names their exported handlers as surfaces under the stems their names give: `get`, `post`, `delete`. The card reads stems `-3 +3`, surfaces `0/4 +3` or thereabouts, and every requirement matched by anchor lands `0 in stem`. That measures a convention the parser has not been taught — a file-path rule of some twenty lines — not a permanent inability, and is stated as such.
- **Treatment, `composer-2.5` × 3.** One `survey` completion more per run, 20–60 s and 10–40 K input tokens. Stem sets identical across the three runs on `cli-jobs`, `express-orders`, `orders`, and `api-routes`; `r9k` the likeliest to drift, because `kafka-consumer` comes from the receiver's type and a model may prefer `consumer` or `messages` despite the convention. The `cli-jobs` risk is `invoice-worker` for the worker on the queue `invoices`, and `nightly-job` for the cron schedule. Surfaces exact on `express-orders` and `orders`; `cli-jobs` may lose the `nightly` command's pair to the schedule's. Recall within one requirement of the control on each of the four parser-covered cases. `api-routes` surfaced per resource — `orders`, `customers`, `health` — with stems exact and recall ≥ 13/16.
- **Treatment, `gpt-5.3-codex` × 3.** Stem agreement the same or better than under `composer-2.5`; the `03-56-54Z` card showed the reasoning model differs enough to matter, and its recall spread is the wider.
- **Budget.** No run on either arm hits a cap at 600/120 under `composer-2.5`; the codex survey turn stays under 200 s. A case that does hit a cap passes at 1200/240.

### Decision rule

- **Treatment wins**: stem sets identical across all three runs on at least four of the five cases, exact against the expected stems on the four parser-covered cases, recall within one requirement of the control, and `api-routes` surfaced per resource. The consequence, for a later branch: the model names and the parser derives; `surface.rs` shrinks to `start()`, `identify()`, and the closure glue, with one scripted `survey` turn per `typescript` scenario in `tests/source.rs` as the price.
- **Control wins**: stem sets differ across runs on two or more cases despite the stated convention — naming needs code.
- **Either way, the likely landing**: the model survey replaces the parser's `unsurfaced` / by-directory fallback, used only where the parser finds no surface. `api-routes` decides that on its own.

### Results

Two checkpoint runs over `api-routes` alone (`EVAL_RUNS=1`, not copied in) preceded the graded runs: the parser arm read `-3 +3`, surfaces `0/4 +7`, and every matched requirement `0 in stem`, as predicted; the model arm read `4/4`, stems `exact`, one `survey` completion of 15 s with no correction round. Nothing was changed after them.

**Decision.** Under `composer-2.5` the treatment meets every clause of the rule: stem sets identical across the three runs on five of five cases (four required), exact against the expected stems on the four parser-covered cases, recall within one requirement of the control on each, and `api-routes` surfaced per resource on every run. Under `gpt-5.3-codex` the survey clauses hold the same and the recall clause cannot be judged for want of a codex control. The likely landing stands: the model survey belongs where the parser finds no surface — `api-routes` shows the parser's fallback naming the HTTP verbs as stems while the model names the resources — and the fuller consequence, the model naming and the parser deriving, is open on this evidence rather than settled against.

Two things the cards show that the predictions did not ask about. The budget ladder never climbed: not one of the forty-five graded runs hit a cap at 600/120, on either arm or model, so the ladder recorded `pass` at the first rung throughout and the longer rungs went unused. And the stable pairs do not make stable ids: the treatment's requirement-id `Stability` on `api-routes` is 0% between runs where the model named the routes `GET /api/customers` and `GET /customers`, because the model-arm surfaces carry no discriminator and `identify()` kebab-cases the whole name into the id (`customers.get-api-customers…` against `customers.get-customers…`). The parser's own route surfaces derive the discriminator from the path with `api` dropped; a model-named surface would need the same derivation — from the name by the same rule, or as one more field of the answer — before its ids could be compared run to run.
